## Overview

Use the Potential Duplicate Users Report to spot accounts that may be duplicates of one another, so you can review them and clean up your records. Duplicate accounts split a person's history, payments, and communication across two profiles, which makes reporting and follow-up harder.

<Callout kind="info">

This report groups accounts that share the same first and last name so you can compare them side by side. Matching names are a signal, not proof, so always review the other details before merging. This report does not include filters or export options.

</Callout>

## Open the Potential Duplicate Users Report

Access the report directly from the Data Check reports area.

<Steps>

  <Step title="Go to the Potential Duplicate Users Report" icon="folder">

  - Sign in with an admin account.
  - In the main navigation, go to **Reports** and select **System**.
  - Under **Data Check**, choose **Potential Duplicate Users**.
  - Confirm the page title reads `Potential Duplicate Users`.

  When the page loads, you see a table with the columns **Name**, **Family ID**, **Status**, **User Type**, **Email**, and **Date Registered**, sorted by name so likely matches sit next to each other.

  </Step>

  <Step title="Compare and resolve duplicates" icon="check-circle">

  - Look for rows that share the same name and appear to be the same person.
  - Compare the **Family ID**, **Email**, and **Date Registered** to confirm whether the accounts are truly duplicates.
  - Select each **Name** to open the profiles and review the full details before merging or archiving the extra record.

  Make all changes from the profiles. The report identifies possible duplicates but does not merge them for you.

  </Step>

</Steps>

## Report columns

Each row is an account that shares a name with at least one other account.

### Name

Account name, shown as `Last, First` and linked to the profile. Two rows with the same name are the pair to compare.

### Family ID

The family the account belongs to. Two accounts with the same Family ID are the same household; different Family IDs may point to separate people who happen to share a name.

### Status

Account status, such as Active, Inactive, or Pending. Use it to decide which record to keep and which to retire.

### User Type

Whether the account is a camper, parent, or another user type. Duplicates are usually the same user type.

### Email

Email address on file. A shared email is a strong sign the accounts are duplicates; different emails may mean they are different people.

### Date Registered

The date and time the account was created, which helps you decide which record is the original to keep.

<Callout kind="alert">

Matching names alone do not confirm a duplicate. Always compare the Family ID, email, and other profile details before merging, so you do not combine two different people.

</Callout>
